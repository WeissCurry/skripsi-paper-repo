# ABSTRACT

The digital investment ecosystem has grown significantly, with crypto assets dominating the *Decentralized Finance* (<Glossary term="DeFi" definition="Decentralized Finance; a blockchain-based financial-services ecosystem that operates without a centralized intermediary.">DeFi</Glossary>) landscape. However, the rising quantity of adoption is not always matched by rational, measurable quality in risk mitigation and Sharia compliance. The Ethereum <Glossary term="staking" definition="The process of locking crypto assets on a Proof-of-Stake network to earn rewards while helping to secure the network.">*staking*</Glossary> ecosystem, largely controlled by third parties, presents complex prospective choices that require an objective evaluation method. This study implements the *The Open Group Architecture Framework Architecture Development Method* (<Glossary term="TOGAF ADM" definition="A standard framework for iterative, structured enterprise-architecture development.">TOGAF ADM</Glossary>) to deliver a structured risk-management architecture design. A descriptive qualitative approach is applied to evaluate *Solo Staking* and <Glossary term="Liquid Staking" definition="A protocol that lets users lock (stake) assets while still receiving a representative, reusable (liquid) token.">*Liquid Staking*</Glossary> (Lido Finance) against the parameters of Fiqh Muamalah. The results show that the conventional *Liquid Staking* protocol violates Sharia eligibility because of its *<Glossary term="rebasing" definition="A mechanism that automatically adjusts a token balance in the user's wallet to reflect rewards or penalties.">rebasing</Glossary>* algorithm, which triggers <Glossary term="Riba al-Nasi'ah" definition="An increase stipulated in a loan or an exchange of ribawi goods on account of a deferral in payment time.">Riba al-Nasi'ah</Glossary>, and its penalty distribution (*<Glossary term="slashing" definition="An automatic financial penalty on a blockchain network against validators that act dishonestly or are inactive.">slashing</Glossary>*), which triggers <Glossary term="Zulm" definition="Injustice or oppression; here, the disproportionate distribution of slashing penalties to blameless investors.">Zulm</Glossary>. To resolve this complexity, the study proposes a business architecture that applies a *Net Asset Value* (<Glossary term="NAV" definition="Net Asset Value; the net value of assets computed as total market value of assets minus liabilities, divided by the number of units.">NAV</Glossary>) model based on the <Glossary term="Wakalah bil Istithmar" definition="An agency contract for investment, in which an agent acts on behalf of the fund owner to manage assets.">Wakalah bil Istithmar</Glossary> contract. Based on a simulation test across 10 risk criteria using data from the 25 April 2026 period, the proposed method proved effective, yielding investment-eligibility stability at *Speculative Grade* with a score of 14. In contrast, the conventional-protocol alternative received an automatic Veto penalty for Sharia non-compliance. The TOGAF ADM framework, carried through to Phase D, proved capable of producing a rational and systematic blueprint that integrates <Glossary term="Smart Contract" definition="A computer program that runs on a blockchain and automatically executes an agreement without an intermediary.">*smart contract*</Glossary> security with the principle of wealth protection (<Glossary term="Hifzul Mal" definition="One of the primary objectives of Maqasid al-Shariah, emphasizing the protection and preservation of property.">Hifzul Mal</Glossary>) in DeFi.

**Keywords:** *Risk Analysis, Enterprise Architecture, Sharia Compliance, Ethereum Staking, TOGAF ADM.*

# 1. INTRODUCTION

Blockchain technology has transformed the global financial landscape through crypto assets, which have shifted from a mere speculative trend to a strategic investment instrument. In Indonesia, crypto assets hold a specific legal position as a lawfully traded commodity (Febrianto et al., 2025; Kindi, 2023). Crypto-asset adoption shows a positive trend, ranking 7th in the world on the global adoption index (Chainalysis Team, 2025), with an investor demographic dominated by the productive 18–35 age group (Shabrina & Sedayu, 2025). However, this rising adoption often raises doubt (syubhat) among Muslim investors about the lawfulness of returns from the digital ecosystem (Rizieq & Baidhowi, 2025). Responding to these dynamics, the Majelis Tarjih dan Tajdid of the Central Board of Muhammadiyah issued an updated fatwa that firmly classifies utility crypto assets as Māl Mutaqawwam, or property of value (Majelis Tarjih dan Tajdid Pimpinan Pusat Muhammadiyah, 2026). The fatwa provides legal certainty that digital financial innovation is in principle permissible (mubah), but strictly prohibits transaction schemes containing interest-bearing debt facilities (crypto lending) or market manipulation.

A risk-management framework in Decentralized Finance (<Glossary term="DeFi" definition="Decentralized Finance; a blockchain-based financial-services ecosystem that operates without a centralized intermediary.">DeFi</Glossary>) becomes crucial because validator vulnerabilities span multiple dimensions. When contextualized with Sharia principles, this risk management relates closely to wealth protection (Hifzul Mal) and compliance risks. Without a comprehensive mitigation architecture, the staking ecosystem is prone to violations of <Glossary term="Gharar" definition="An element of uncertainty, excessive speculation, or lack of clarity in a contract or transaction.">Gharar</Glossary> (uncertainty) and Zulm (injustice) in the distribution of operational penalties (Izadin, Mohd. Yusof, & Mazlan, 2025). Intrinsically, however, a validator's active participation in the PoS system (as in Solo Staking) has characteristics aligned with Islamic principles, because it can be represented as a Ju'alah contract, or a purely performance-based reward (Habib & Jawa, 2024).

Several prior literature studies have examined the elements of this ecosystem separately. Adamyk et al. (2025) formulated a conceptual DeFi risk framework that maps vulnerabilities into technological, operational, financial, and compliance dimensions. From the Sharia perspective, Habib & Jawa (2024) examined the validity of PoS transaction validation as a Ju'alah contract, while Izadin et al. (2025) affirmed that digital-portfolio mitigation aligns strongly with Maqasid al-Shariah principles in curbing speculation. On protocol-performance evaluation, Xiong et al. (2025) identified that the Liquid Staking mechanism (such as Lido's stETH) is prone to triggering cascading-liquidation contagion risk during asset devaluation. To bridge the complexity of blockchain systems, research by Judijanto et al. (2023) and Oak Kyaw Zaw et al. (2025) demonstrated that using an Enterprise Architecture (EA) approach is highly effective for creating transparency, ensuring data integrity, and enforcing security standards on decentralized systems.

Although these literature components complement one another, no single study has yet successfully integrated the three crucial domains — technical governance of blockchain technology, comprehensive risk management, and Sharia-compliance standardization — into one complete information-systems architecture blueprint. Existing literature tends either to focus purely on technical risk analysis without offering an architectural overhaul, or conversely to be only a Fiqh Muamalah review without detailing a technical design implementable in a <Glossary term="Smart Contract" definition="A computer program that runs on a blockchain and automatically executes an agreement without an intermediary.">Smart Contract</Glossary>. This void is an urgent <Glossary term="research gap" definition="A gap in the scholarly literature where a problem has not yet been answered satisfactorily.">research gap</Glossary> that needs to be explored.

Starting from this problem, the study aims to design a comprehensive risk-management and Sharia-compliance architecture for the Ethereum staking ecosystem. The research method is descriptive qualitative, through a literature study (examining the parameters of DSN-MUI fatwas and the AAOIFI standard) and actual observation of <Glossary term="on-chain" definition="Data permanently recorded on the blockchain ledger.">on-chain</Glossary> data platforms on the Ethereum <Glossary term="Mainnet" definition="The main network of a blockchain protocol where real transactions occur and carry economic value.">mainnet</Glossary> (via Etherscan, Dune Analytics, and Rated Network). A comparative study is applied to two main mechanisms: Solo Staking (self-managed) and conventional Liquid Staking. To bridge normative theological requirements into the system's technical execution logic, the study adopts the design method of The Open Group Architecture Framework Architecture Development Method (TOGAF ADM). This iterative framework is applied sequentially, bounded from the Sharia-eligibility initiation stage (Preliminary Phase), Architecture Vision (Phase A), Business Architecture (Phase B), Information Systems Architecture (Phase C), through Technology Architecture (Phase D).

Data collection is divided into two technical stages: literature study and system observation.

The literature study was conducted to compile the Sharia-compliance parameters and the risk matrix. Normative references were drawn from DSN-MUI fatwas (No. 116/2017 and No. 52/2006) and AAOIFI Shari'ah Standard No. 57 to determine the tolerance limits for speculation. Meanwhile, the technical parameters were based on the risk-identification clauses of ISO 31000:2018 and decentralized-governance risk frameworks.

Observation was performed directly on the technical documentation (<Glossary term="Whitepaper" definition="The official document that explains the technology, vision, and operating mechanism of a blockchain project.">Whitepaper</Glossary>) of the protocol entities and the Ethereum Foundation. On-chain execution data was monitored using public analytics tools: Etherscan to trace transaction trails, Dune Analytics and Rated Network to pull historical validator uptime data, and DefiLlama to measure the asset centralization ratio.

## 3.2. Business Architecture

Business-process modeling was applied to evaluate the current operating mechanism and to design the proposed target. Evaluating the baseline architecture shows that the Solo Staking mechanism fundamentally meets Sharia rules through a Ju'alah contract purely based on the real performance of securing the network.

Conversely, analysis of Liquid Staking on the Lido Finance protocol identifies a Sharia violation caused by modifying the return-distribution algorithm. The automatic token-addition algorithm (Positive Rebase) triggers the practice of Riba al-Nasi'ah by creating fictitious value without an underlying exchange. In addition, executing a flat deduction (Negative Rebase) triggers the practice of Zulm (injustice), because it distributes operational penalty fines (slashing) to all contributors regardless of where the fault lies.

The proposed target reconstructs this governance by applying a Net Asset Value (NAV) model based on the Wakalah bil Istithmar contract. In this architecture, the balance-quantity manipulation algorithm is replaced with a mechanism that updates the valuation of the underlying asset. Losses and gains are reflected proportionally in the token price ratio, so executing a penalty fine does not unilaterally harm the remaining members of the fund-pooling unit (pool).

## 3.3. Information Systems and Technology Architecture

In the data architecture design (Phase C), the information structure is configured in the Smart Contract database to record the underlying asset value without manipulating the number of tokens in circulation. The application layer is designed in four parts: the user interface, off-chain services, on-chain smart contracts, and the validator server infrastructure.

# CONCLUSION

Based on the analysis and design described, it is concluded that the Solo Staking mechanism meets Sharia principles through a Ju'alah contract purely based on the real work of data validation. Conversely, the conventional Liquid Staking protocol is identified as violating fiqh provisions. The violation stems from implementing an automatic balance-addition algorithm that triggers the practice of Riba al-Nasi'ah, together with a flat operational-penalty distribution mechanism that triggers Zulm by harming parties outside the offending entity.

Applying the proposed architecture using the Net Asset Value (NAV) model grounded in the Wakalah bil Istithmar contract proved effective in fundamentally resolving these violations. Modifying the mechanism toward organic asset-valuation updates successfully prevents balance manipulation, so the principle of wealth protection (Hifzul Mal) can be implemented into the smart contract logic. Through integrating the Risk Explorer dashboard and enforcing an E-Contract, this architecture produces a more transparent and measurable risk governance.

This study is limited to applying the TOGAF ADM cycle ending at Phase D (Technology Architecture). In addition, the security parameters of the proposed smart contract are still at Verified Code status and have not undergone a third-party firm audit. The metric data used for the risk-assessment simulation is also static, based on a snapshot of a specific observation window. Therefore, further research is advised to conduct direct field trials (stress testing) on a test network (Testnet), carry out a thorough code security audit, and continue the architecture stages toward Phase E (Opportunities & Solutions) through Phase G (Implementation Governance) to produce a complete physical-infrastructure migration guide.
